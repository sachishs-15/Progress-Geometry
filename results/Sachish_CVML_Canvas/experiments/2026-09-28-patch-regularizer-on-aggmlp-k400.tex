\subsection{Regularizer Comparison: No Regularizer vs.\ SigReg vs.\ VICReg (K400 10\%, Patch-Channel)}
\label{exp:regularizer-comparison-k400}

\paragraph{What we tried} Three patch-channel PWM checkpoints on the K400 10\% subset
(\texttt{window\_size=0}, \texttt{batch\_size=16}, \texttt{straighten\_weight=0.1}
throughout), varying only the anti-collapse regularizer:
\texttt{pwm\_patch\_channel\_k400\_10pct\_noreg\_window0\_batch16\_1} (\texttt{noreg},
no regularizer at all), \texttt{pwm\_patch\_channel\_k400\_10pct\_sigreg0.1\_window0\_batch16}
(\texttt{sigreg0.1}, \texttt{sigreg\_weight=0.1}), and
\texttt{pwm\_patch\_channel\_k400\_10pct\_vicreg\_window0\_batch16\_1} (\texttt{sw0.1
(vicreg)}, \texttt{vicreg\_std\_weight=1.0}, \texttt{vicreg\_cov\_weight=0.04}). A further
\texttt{straighten\_weight} sweep on top of the VICReg config
(\texttt{sw0.5}--\texttt{sw2.0}) exists but isn't broken out here. Scored the three core
checkpoints with the same full metric suite from Section~\ref{sec:methodology} (Main and
Additional), and compared training-loss trajectories from epoch 0 to each run's selected
best epoch.

\paragraph{Inference}

\textbf{This directly resolves the open question from the window-size/VICReg ablation.}
That experiment found VICReg-on-token-granularity made no difference to collapse no
matter the window size, and flagged the likely cause: VICReg was regularizing the raw
per-patch tensor, not $z_{\mathrm{agg}}$, the tensor the straightening loss and every
collapse metric actually operate on (Section~\ref{sec:methodology-architecture}). This
experiment tests that diagnosis directly by moving the same regularizer weights onto
$z_{\mathrm{agg}}$ instead, on a fresh \texttt{noreg} baseline for the same architecture.

\textbf{The fix works --- \texttt{effective\_rank\_fraction} goes from collapsed to
healthier than either DINOv2 baseline.} \texttt{noreg} reproduces the same severe
collapse as every checkpoint in the previous experiment (0.0084, red). Regularizing
$z_{\mathrm{agg}}$ directly changes that completely: \texttt{sigreg0.1} reaches 0.8115
(a near-exact tie with DINOv2\textsubscript{CLS}'s 0.8120, left uncolored) and
\texttt{sw0.1 (vicreg)} reaches 0.9756, green --- clearly beating both DINOv2 baselines,
the first trained checkpoint in this log to do so.

\textbf{Fixing collapse flips every straightness-family metric from green to red ---
the mirror image of the previous experiment's blind spot.} \texttt{noreg} keeps the same
pattern seen throughout the window/VICReg ablation: \texttt{trajectory\_straightness}
(0.2178), \texttt{mean\_velocity\_cosine} (-0.0416), and \texttt{direction\_reversal\_fraction}
(0.5139) all beat both DINOv2 baselines, green, while collapsed. Once collapse is fixed,
those same three metrics go red for both \texttt{sigreg0.1} and \texttt{sw0.1 (vicreg)}
(\texttt{trajectory\_straightness} drops to 0.082, \texttt{direction\_reversal\_fraction}
rises to 0.985--0.988, near the i.i.d.-noise reference of 1.0). \texttt{train\_straighten}
itself is flat-to-worse for both regularized runs even as \texttt{train\_total} falls
sharply --- the optimizer satisfies collapse-avoidance by adopting a noisier, more
reversal-prone trajectory, not a smoother one. This is the sharpest confirmation yet that
straightness and collapse-avoidance compete for the same weight budget rather than both
improving together, and that a straightness number alone --- healthy or not --- says
nothing about whether the representation is collapsed.

\textbf{Unlike straightness, \texttt{time\_progress\_spearman} and
\texttt{distance\_to\_goal\_spearman} stay red for all three checkpoints, fixed collapse
or not.} \texttt{time\_progress\_spearman} rises with each regularizer (0.475 $\to$
0.737 $\to$ 0.762) but never gets close to either DINOv2 baseline (0.90--0.91);
\texttt{distance\_to\_goal\_spearman} barely moves at all (0.332--0.365, vs.\ 0.61--0.62
baseline) and is actually \emph{lowest} for \texttt{sigreg0.1}, the middle point of the
three. So fixing \texttt{effective\_rank\_fraction} buys real gains on
\texttt{time\_progress\_spearman} without buying anything close to frozen-encoder-level
temporal ordering --- rank, straightness, and ordinality are three separate axes here,
not two.

\textbf{None of the three reach frozen-encoder territory on chirality, regardless of
collapse status.} \texttt{chirality.mean\_accuracy} is red for all three (0.52--0.58,
vs.\ 0.78--0.80 for both DINOv2 baselines) --- fixing rank collapse did not recover
semantic/directional content on this probe specifically, unlike V-JEPA2.1 in the
previous experiment, which combined low rank with above-baseline semantic probes. Fixing
collapse is necessary but not sufficient here.
