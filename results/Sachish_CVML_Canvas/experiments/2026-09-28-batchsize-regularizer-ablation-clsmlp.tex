\subsection{Batch Size $\times$ Regularizer Ablation (CLS-MLP, K400)}
\label{exp:bs-regularizer-clsmlp}

\paragraph{What we tried} Six CLS residual-MLP predictive-world-model checkpoints on
K400 (DINOv2 CLS features $\to$ residual-MLP encoder, \texttt{token\_mode=cls},
\texttt{window\_size=0}: \texttt{pwm\_cls\_residual\_mlp\_paper\_window0}[\_sigreg/\_vicreg]
for batch size 32, \texttt{pwm\_cls\_residual\_mlp\_bs512}[\_sigreg/\_vicreg] for batch
size 512), crossing batch size (32 vs.\ 512) with regularizer (none, SigReg, VICReg),
\texttt{straighten\_weight=0.1} throughout --- the same weight used in the
patch-channel family. Scored the same full metric suite from
Section~\ref{sec:methodology} (Main and Additional), and compared each loss component
from epoch 0 to that run's selected best epoch (out of 49 logged).

\paragraph{Inference}

\textbf{This checks whether the collapse-vs-straightness tension seen in the last three
experiments is specific to the patch-channel architecture, or shows up in a completely
different encoder too.} All three prior experiments used \texttt{token\_mode=patch},
\texttt{projector\_type=channel} --- a spatial, multi-token-per-frame representation.
This experiment switches to a CLS residual-MLP encoder (one token per frame by
construction, no aggregation head, no \texttt{vicreg\_granularity} choice to get wrong)
and crosses batch size (32 vs.\ 512) with the same three regularizer settings.

\textbf{Collapse without regularization is far milder here, and \texttt{effective\_rank\_fraction}
gets colored on both ends.} Both no-regularizer configs (bs32:none 0.731, bs512:none
0.771) sit close to \emph{both} DINOv2 baselines --- bs32:none is red (just below the
lower baseline), bs512:none is uncolored (between the two, mixed) --- nothing close to
the patch-channel \texttt{noreg} checkpoint's 0.008--0.018 red-across-the-board collapse
two experiments ago. All four regularized configs are green, clearly beating both
baselines (0.815--0.838). Regularization still helps here, but it's the difference
between "fine" and "slightly better," not "collapsed" and "healthy."

\textbf{The same straightness/regularization tension still shows up, in miniature, with
the colors flipping the same way as every patch-channel experiment.}
\texttt{trajectory\_straightness} is green for both no-regularizer configs (bs32:none
0.1563, bs512:none 0.1492) and red for all four regularized ones (0.130--0.132);
\texttt{direction\_reversal\_fraction} follows the identical pattern in the same
direction. This is the same tension confirmed independently in a different encoder
architecture, just at much smaller magnitude since nothing here starts severely
collapsed.

\textbf{bs512:none is the first --- and so far only --- trained checkpoint in this log to
beat both baselines on \texttt{chirality.mean\_accuracy}.} 0.8070, green, ahead of
DINOv2\textsubscript{CLS}'s 0.7993 and DINOv2\textsubscript{mean}'s 0.7763, while also
sitting in the uncolored middle ground on \texttt{effective\_rank\_fraction} (0.771,
not collapsed) and green on \texttt{trajectory\_straightness}/\texttt{direction\_reversal\_fraction}.
Every regularized config in this whole log so far has traded chirality away for fixed
collapse (Section~\ref{exp:regularizer-comparison-k400}: 0.52--0.58, all red); this is
the first checkpoint to look reasonable on rank, straightness, \emph{and} chirality at
once, without an explicit anti-collapse regularizer at all.

\textbf{The two temporal-ordering metrics dissociate in opposite directions, and this
time \texttt{distance\_to\_goal\_spearman} sides with the no-regularizer configs, not
against them.} \texttt{time\_progress\_spearman} improves with regularization (bs32:none
0.843 and bs512:none 0.864, both red; all four regularized configs 0.906--0.908,
uncolored --- close to baseline but not quite over 0.9114). \texttt{distance\_to\_goal\_spearman}
moves the opposite way: bs32:none (0.629) and bs512:none (0.632) are \emph{green},
clearly beating both DINOv2 baselines, while all four regularized configs are red
(0.583--0.594). So the same two checkpoints that win on chirality and straightness ---
the no-regularizer configs --- also win on \texttt{distance\_to\_goal\_spearman}
specifically, even though they lose on \texttt{time\_progress\_spearman}. Regularization
buys classical ordinal ranking here at a direct cost to the goal-distance signal, the
same shape of trade-off seen in the straighten-weight sweep, just split across two
different checkpoint families instead of one continuous dial.

\textbf{\texttt{train\_straighten} never meaningfully improves in any of the six --- this
is now the norm for this recipe, not an isolated outlier.} The largest movement is
$-1\%$; two configs (bs32:sigreg, bs512:vicreg) end up worse than they started. This
matches the sw0.1 failure case from the previous experiment exactly, but shown here
across an entire family regardless of encoder architecture, batch size, or regularizer
choice --- \texttt{straighten\_weight=0.1} against \texttt{pred\_weight=1.0} appears
systematically too weak to make real progress on its own objective in this recipe,
independent of everything else tested across this log.

\textbf{Two configs select a very early best epoch --- worth checking directly.}
bs32:none (epoch 7) and bs32:vicreg (epoch 1) both select their best checkpoint far
earlier than the other four (epoch 48--49), echoing the early-divergence pattern from
the window/VICReg ablation two experiments back, though without that experiment's
post-best-epoch loss values to confirm whether these two diverge afterward or simply
plateau.
