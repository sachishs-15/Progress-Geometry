\paragraph{Inference}

\textbf{This extends the previous experiment's working \texttt{sw0.1 (vicreg)}
checkpoint (the one that beat both DINOv2 baselines on \texttt{effective\_rank\_fraction})
across a range of \texttt{straighten\_weight}, to see whether that fix holds as the
straightening objective is pushed harder.} All else is fixed at the previous
experiment's winning recipe (VICReg on $z_{\mathrm{agg}}$); only \texttt{sw} varies.

\textbf{Rank collapses back in as \texttt{sw} rises, in a clean, monotonic trade against
straightness --- the same tension as the last two experiments, now as a continuous
dial rather than three discrete points.} \texttt{effective\_rank\_fraction} is green
(beats both baselines) only at sw0.1 (0.964) and sw0.5 (0.923); by sw1.0 it has fallen
to 0.631, red, and keeps falling to 0.201 by sw2.0 --- back into the same collapsed
territory as the un-fixed window/VICReg ablation two experiments ago.
\texttt{trajectory\_straightness} moves the opposite way: red (below both baselines) at
sw0.1--0.5, green from sw1.0 onward. \texttt{direction\_reversal\_fraction} and
\texttt{mean\_velocity\_cosine} track the same crossover, flipping from red to green
between sw0.1 and sw0.5. There is no \texttt{sw} in this sweep where collapse and
straightness are both simultaneously green.

\textbf{sw0.1 is a genuine failure point, not just the conservative end of a trend.} Its
\texttt{train\_straighten} at epoch 49 (1.410) is \emph{worse} than its own epoch-0 value
(1.398) --- the optimizer effectively abandons the straightening objective at this
weight even as \texttt{train\_pred} and the VICReg terms all improve substantially in the
same run. This is also the only point in the sweep where \texttt{effective\_rank\_fraction}
is healthiest (0.964) yet every straightness-family metric is red and worst-in-sweep ---
the cleanest single data point yet for "collapse-avoidance and straightening compete for
the same weight budget," now visible within a single fixed architecture and regularizer,
varying only one scalar.

\textbf{Chirality never turns green anywhere in the sweep, unlike some frozen encoders.}
0.556--0.662 across all five points, well below both DINOv2 baselines throughout ---
unlike V-JEPA2.1 (Section~\ref{tab:encoder-comparison-ssv2-main}), which combined a
straightness win with above-baseline semantic probes, no point on this sweep recovers
chirality even where \texttt{effective\_rank\_fraction} is healthy.

\textbf{\texttt{time\_progress\_spearman} and \texttt{distance\_to\_goal\_spearman} are
red at every single point in the sweep --- unlike straightness, there is no \texttt{sw}
here that reaches frozen-encoder-level ordinality.} \texttt{time\_progress\_spearman}
peaks at sw0.5 (0.896) and \emph{declines} through sw1.0--2.0 (0.812 $\to$ 0.738 $\to$
0.692), even as \texttt{trajectory\_straightness} keeps climbing through sw2.0 --- but
even that sw0.5 peak stays below both DINOv2 baselines (0.90--0.91). A sweep tuned to
maximize classical ordinality would stop at sw0.5; one tuned to maximize local
straightness hasn't peaked by sw2.0 and would push further --- these are not
interchangeable optimization targets, and neither one reaches baseline-level temporal
ordering anywhere in this sweep. \texttt{distance\_to\_goal\_spearman} tells a similar
story with a different peak: it rises from 0.409 (sw0.1) to roughly 0.55 at sw0.5--1.0,
then declines again to 0.489 by sw2.0, staying red (well below the 0.61--0.62 baseline)
throughout. Both temporal-order metrics agree the sweet spot is somewhere in the
sw0.5--1.0 range, not at either extreme --- but neither gets this checkpoint family
close to what a frozen encoder achieves on ordinality alone.
