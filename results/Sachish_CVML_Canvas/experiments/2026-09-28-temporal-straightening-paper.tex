\subsection{Window Size vs.\ VICReg Ablation (Patch-Channel PWM)}
\label{exp:window-vicreg-ablation}

\paragraph{What we tried} The patch-channel predictive world model had been collapsing
in earlier runs, so we adopted the anti-collapse recipe from
\cite{wang2026temporal_straightening} directly --- a VICReg std/cov regularizer applied
alongside the curvature/straightening loss --- and swept window size (0 vs.\ 3) to check
whether matching their setup would fix it. Four checkpoints, all \texttt{straighten\_weight=0.1},
\texttt{batch\_size=32}, \texttt{vicreg\_granularity=token}: \texttt{pwm\_patch\_channel\_paper\_window0\_2}
(\texttt{window0\_2}, normal), \texttt{pwm\_patch\_channel\_paper\_window0\_vicreg\_3}
(\texttt{window0\_vicreg\_3}, VICReg), \texttt{pwm\_patch\_channel\_paper\_window3}
(\texttt{window3}, normal), \texttt{pwm\_patch\_channel\_paper\_window3\_vicreg}
(\texttt{window3\_vicreg}, VICReg). Scored with the same full metric suite from
Section~\ref{sec:methodology} (Main and Additional), and separately compared training
dynamics across all four.

\paragraph{Inference}

\textbf{This is the first attempt at actually training the patch-channel architecture
whose frozen-encoder relatives (DINOv2\textsubscript{mean}, V-JEPA2\textsubscript{mean})
looked reasonable in the previous experiment.} The patch-channel predictive world model
had been collapsing in earlier runs, so this ablation adopted a published anti-collapse
recipe (VICReg std/cov on top of the curvature/straightening loss) and swept window size
to see whether matching that setup would fix it.

\textbf{It did not, and every checkpoint here is colored red on collapse for the same
reason.} All four checkpoints land at \texttt{effective\_rank\_fraction}
0.0155--0.0185 --- 40--50$\times$ lower than either DINOv2 baseline (0.73--0.81) and
far below even the worst frozen encoder in the previous experiment
(V-JEPA2\textsubscript{mean}, 0.4202). Window size (0 vs.\ 3) and VICReg (on vs.\ off)
make essentially no difference to this number --- whatever is causing the collapse, it
isn't what this ablation swept.

\textbf{Meanwhile every straightness/reversal/velocity-cosine cell is green, and
\texttt{time\_progress\_spearman}/\texttt{distance\_to\_goal\_spearman} are both
red --- the exact blind spot this document's own framing warns about.}
\texttt{trajectory\_straightness} (0.223--0.236) beats \emph{both} DINOv2 baselines for
all four checkpoints, as does \texttt{mean\_velocity\_cosine} (-0.045 to -0.008, i.e.\
far less negative/zig-zagging than DINOv2's -0.24 to -0.26) and
\texttt{direction\_reversal\_fraction} (0.50--0.52, close to the random-walk reference of
0.5, vs.\ DINOv2's 0.88--0.89, close to the i.i.d.-noise reference of 1.0). Read in
isolation, these four checkpoints would look like the straightest, smoothest
trajectories in this entire log. Read next to \texttt{effective\_rank\_fraction} --- or
next to the two temporal-order metrics, both red for all four checkpoints
(\texttt{time\_progress\_spearman} 0.46--0.49 vs.\ DINOv2's 0.90--0.91;
\texttt{distance\_to\_goal\_spearman} 0.34--0.37 vs.\ 0.61--0.62) --- they are also the
most collapsed and the least temporally ordered checkpoints in the log so far. This is
the clearest demonstration yet of why straightness alone cannot be read as "this
representation is good": \texttt{chirality.mean\_accuracy} (0.565--0.616, all red, well
below both baselines' 0.78--0.80) is a third independent signal pointing the same
direction as rank and temporal order --- the straightness family of metrics is the odd
one out here, silent on the collapse or actively misleading.

\textbf{window\_size, not VICReg, determines whether training completes cleanly.} Both
window0 runs (\texttt{window0\_2}, \texttt{window0\_vicreg\_3}) hit their best epoch
early (16, 18) and then get \emph{worse} for the remaining $\sim$30 epochs of the same
50-epoch run, regardless of VICReg; both window3 runs reach their best epoch late (43,
49) and stay near it. VICReg being on or off does not change this pattern, which is a
first hint that VICReg is not doing the job it was added to do here --- pursued directly
in the next experiment.
