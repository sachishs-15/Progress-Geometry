\subsection{Encoder Comparison: SSv2 vs.\ Ego4D GoalStep}
\label{exp:encoder-comparison-ssv2-ego4d}

\paragraph{What we tried} Scored eight frozen pretrained encoder/pooling variants
(DINOv2 CLS and mean-pooled patch, DINOv3, V-JEPA2 and V-JEPA2.1 mean-pooled, CLIP,
SigLIP, LevlJEPA) on SSv2 validation with the full \texttt{encoder\_planning\_eval}
metric suite, and separately scored DINOv2 (CLS) on Ego4D GoalStep validation, to check
whether the same encoder's latent geometry holds up on a different, longer-form
procedural video dataset. Metrics follow the Main/Additional segregation and $x$
(raw)/$z$ (L2-normalized) convention defined in Section~\ref{sec:methodology}.
CLIP/SigLIP have no train split prepared yet, so their semantic-probe and
cross-clip-consistency rows are not available. \textbf{DINOv2\textsubscript{CLS} and
DINOv2\textsubscript{mean} are not two poolings of the same checkpoint}: CLS uses
\texttt{facebook/dinov2-with-registers-small} (register tokens enabled), while mean uses
\texttt{facebook/dinov2-small}, the no-register-token checkpoint (see
\texttt{experiments/frozen\_encoders/models/dinov2/model.py}) --- so any difference
between those two columns reflects both the pooling change and the registers/no-registers
checkpoint difference confounded together, not pooling alone.

\paragraph{Inference}

\textbf{This is the reference point the rest of this log measures against, not a
follow-up to anything.} DINOv2\textsubscript{CLS} and DINOv2\textsubscript{mean} are
used as the baseline pair throughout: both are frozen, untrained, off-the-shelf, and
score well on the metrics this project cares about most
(\texttt{effective\_rank\_fraction} 0.73--0.81, no collapse; \texttt{chirality.mean\_accuracy}
0.78--0.80). Elsewhere in this log, \good{green} marks a cell that beats \emph{both}
baselines on a metric with a well-defined better direction, by a margin that looks like
a real effect rather than noise; \bad{red} marks the opposite. Mixed results (better
than one baseline, worse than the other) and modest differences are left uncolored.

\textbf{The collapse-vs-straightness tension this whole project tracks is already
visible among frozen encoders, before any training happens.} V-JEPA2\textsubscript{mean}
beats both DINOv2 baselines on \texttt{trajectory\_straightness},
\texttt{direction\_reversal\_fraction}, and \texttt{mean\_velocity\_cosine} --- yet has
by far the worst \texttt{effective\_rank\_fraction} in the table (0.4202). V-JEPA2.1
shows the same pattern in miniature (straightness/reversal/velocity-cosine all beat
baseline, \texttt{effective\_rank\_fraction}=0.588, also below both DINOv2 variants).
Both are the same architecture family evaluated completely untrained by this project ---
so a representation that looks locally straighter than DINOv2 while carrying less usable
rank is not something training introduces later in this log; it is already latent in how
these encoders' raw features are structured. \texttt{time\_progress\_spearman} agrees
with the rank story rather than the straightness story for both (red, 0.883--0.893, vs.\
DINOv2's 0.904--0.911), but \texttt{distance\_to\_goal\_spearman} splits the two: V-JEPA2.1
beats both baselines (0.6289, green) while V-JEPA2 lands in between (0.6171, uncolored)
--- the two "temporal order" metrics don't even agree with each other on V-JEPA2.1 here.
It's also worth noting V-JEPA2.1 does \emph{not} come at zero semantic cost the way the
collapsed trained checkpoints later in this log do: its \texttt{change.linear.balanced\_acc}
(0.4670) and \texttt{content+change.linear.balanced\_acc} (0.5413) both exceed both
DINOv2 baselines too, so whatever V-JEPA2.1's lower rank costs it, it isn't showing up as
lost semantic content on these probes specifically.

\textbf{DINOv3 is a near-exact tie with DINOv2\textsubscript{CLS} on collapse, not an
improvement.} \texttt{effective\_rank\_fraction} 0.8123 vs.\ 0.8120 is within noise ---
deliberately left uncolored despite the nominal edge, and every other collapse/straightness
metric favors DINOv2\textsubscript{CLS} outright.

\textbf{On \texttt{time\_progress\_spearman} specifically, DINOv2\textsubscript{CLS}/mean
are the best in the table, and nothing else comes close.} Every other encoder is red
(CLIP 0.8729, SigLIP 0.8823, LevlJEPA 0.8912, V-JEPA2 0.8827, V-JEPA2.1 0.8926) except
DINOv3, an uncolored near-tie at 0.9113. \texttt{distance\_to\_goal\_spearman} is less
one-sided: most encoders are red there too, but V-JEPA2.1 (0.6289) clearly beats both
baselines and V-JEPA2 (0.6171) is close enough to land uncolored --- so whatever V-JEPA
family encoders lose on classical ordinal ranking, they partly make back on
distance-to-goal specifically.

\textbf{LevlJEPA is the weakest encoder on semantic content despite reasonable rank.}
\texttt{effective\_rank\_fraction}=0.802 (healthy, close to DINOv2\textsubscript{CLS}'s
0.812), yet \texttt{chirality.mean\_accuracy} (0.7147, colored red) and
\texttt{content.knn.balanced\_acc} (0.1478, the lowest of any encoder with a train split
available) show healthy rank alone does not guarantee semantic content survives.

\textbf{The same frozen DINOv2\textsubscript{CLS} encoder looks far less ordinal on
Ego4D than on SSv2.} \texttt{time\_progress\_spearman} drops from 0.9040 (SSv2) to
0.6019 (Ego4D), and \texttt{distance\_to\_goal\_spearman} from 0.6110 to 0.2290 ---
\texttt{effective\_rank\_fraction} stays reasonably healthy on both (0.8120 vs.\ 0.7655),
so this is not collapse, it is Ego4D's longer, less visually self-similar procedural
clips giving the encoder's raw geometry less of a clean temporal-progress signal to
begin with. Any later experiment that evaluates on Ego4D should not expect SSv2-level
ordinality numbers even from a healthy encoder.
