\subsection{Encoder-Only Output, Fixed Pooling (Agg-Granularity Regularizer Comparison)}
\label{exp:encoder-only-pooling-agg}

\paragraph{What we tried} Section~\ref{exp:regularizer-comparison-k400} fixed the
window/VICReg ablation's collapse by moving the regularizer onto $z_{\mathrm{agg}}$
instead of the raw per-patch tensor, and Section~\ref{exp:encoder-only-pooling} then
asked, for the \emph{un-fixed} token-granularity checkpoints, whether the encoder's own
raw output was ever actually collapsed or whether the (under-regularized) aggregation
head was doing the damage. This experiment asks the same question of the
\emph{already-fixed} agg-granularity checkpoints: \texttt{noreg},
\texttt{sigreg0.1}, and \texttt{sw0.1 (vicreg)} from
Section~\ref{exp:regularizer-comparison-k400}
(\texttt{pwm\_patch\_channel\_k400\_10pct\_noreg\_window0\_batch16\_1},
\texttt{..\_sigreg0.1\_window0\_batch16}, \texttt{..\_vicreg\_window0\_batch16\_1}, each
at its own best epoch --- 115, 113, 113). $z = \texttt{model.encoder}(x)$ was pooled with
the same fixed, untrained \texttt{mean}/\texttt{max}/\texttt{concat} reductions used in
Section~\ref{exp:encoder-only-pooling}, bypassing \texttt{model.aggregate}(z) --- the
trained AggregationHead these checkpoints' own regularizer was actually pointed at,
unlike the token-granularity family. Computed fully in memory, no intermediate feature
file written to disk. Scored with the same full metric suite from
Section~\ref{sec:methodology} (Main and Additional).

\paragraph{Inference}

\textbf{Unlike the token-granularity family, \texttt{noreg} here is already healthy on
collapse under \texttt{mean}/\texttt{max} pooling without any regularizer at all --- and
the two regularizers make it \emph{worse}, not better.} \texttt{effective\_rank\_fraction}
is green for \texttt{noreg} under both \texttt{mean} (0.8176) and \texttt{max} (0.8867);
\texttt{sigreg0.1} and \texttt{vicreg} are uncolored under \texttt{mean} (close to
baseline but not clearly beating it) and green under \texttt{max}, but never ahead of
\texttt{noreg}'s own numbers at the same pooling. This is the opposite of what these
regularizers were added for: on this specific tensor (the raw encoder output, not
$z_{\mathrm{agg}}$, which is what they were actually trained against), a checkpoint with
no explicit anti-collapse term at all has as healthy or healthier rank than either
regularized one.

\textbf{Under \texttt{concat} specifically, the regularized checkpoints lose the
straightness-family wins that \texttt{noreg} keeps.} \texttt{noreg} stays green on
\texttt{trajectory\_straightness}, \texttt{mean\_velocity\_cosine}, and
\texttt{direction\_reversal\_fraction} under \texttt{concat}, matching the pattern seen
throughout this log. \texttt{sigreg0.1} and \texttt{vicreg} flip all three to red under
\texttt{concat} --- \texttt{direction\_reversal\_fraction} in particular reaches
0.9713 (sigreg0.1) and 0.9273 (vicreg), close to the i.i.d.-noise reference of 1.0,
noticeably worse than \texttt{noreg}'s 0.8562. Trained with a regularizer that was
supposed to make the representation healthier, these two checkpoints instead show more
locally chaotic per-patch geometry than the unregularized baseline, on the exact tensor
their own training never directly saw.

\textbf{\texttt{distance\_to\_goal\_spearman} still only turns green under
\texttt{concat}, confirming the pattern from the token-granularity family holds
regardless of where the regularizer was applied.} All three checkpoints are red under
\texttt{mean} and \texttt{max} (0.36--0.52), and \texttt{noreg}/\texttt{vicreg} turn
green under \texttt{concat} (0.6493, 0.6290) while \texttt{sigreg0.1} stays red (0.5832,
the one exception --- also the checkpoint whose \texttt{concat} numbers look worst
across the board here). Preserving patch structure rather than pooling still looks like
the more important lever for this specific metric than which regularizer, or whether
any regularizer, was used.

\textbf{None of the nine cells reach frozen-encoder-level chirality, and content probes
stay weak except under \texttt{concat}.} \texttt{chirality.mean\_accuracy} is red for
every checkpoint under every pooling (0.58--0.74, vs.\ 0.78--0.80 baseline).
\texttt{content.linear.balanced\_acc} climbs noticeably under \texttt{concat}
(0.20--0.23) compared to \texttt{mean}/\texttt{max} (0.07--0.09) for all three
checkpoints alike --- the same content-preservation-via-concat pattern seen in the
token-granularity comparison, independent of which regularizer (if any) trained the
checkpoint.

\textbf{Net read: for this specific agg-granularity family, SigReg and VICReg's benefit
was entirely on $z_{\mathrm{agg}}$ --- read through the raw encoder output instead, they
add no visible benefit and measurably hurt straightness-family metrics under
\texttt{concat}.} This is a useful boundary condition on Section~\ref{exp:regularizer-comparison-k400}'s
fix: it made $z_{\mathrm{agg}}$ healthy, but did not make the upstream encoder
representation itself any healthier than an unregularized encoder already was --- the
regularizer's effect appears to be localized to the tensor it was actually computed on,
not something that propagates back to make the encoder's own per-patch output better in
any way this evaluation can see.
