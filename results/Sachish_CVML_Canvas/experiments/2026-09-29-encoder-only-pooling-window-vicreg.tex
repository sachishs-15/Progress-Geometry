\subsection{Encoder-Only Output, Fixed Pooling (Bypassing the Trained Aggregation Head)}
\label{exp:encoder-only-pooling}

\paragraph{What we tried} The window/VICReg ablation found severe collapse
(\texttt{effective\_rank\_fraction} 0.0155--0.0185) reading every checkpoint through
$z_{\mathrm{agg}} = \texttt{model.aggregate}(z)$ --- the learned AggregationHead's
output, the tensor the curvature/VICReg losses and every eval metric in that experiment
actually see. This experiment asks whether that collapse was already present in
$z = \texttt{model.encoder}(x)$ itself (the raw, per-patch, channel-projected tokens,
$(B,T,P{=}196,\text{out\_dim}{=}8)$, \emph{before} the aggregation head ever touches it),
or whether the aggregation head is contributing to or amplifying it. Same four
checkpoints as the window/VICReg ablation
(\texttt{pwm\_patch\_channel\_paper\_window0\_2}, \texttt{..\_window0\_vicreg\_3},
\texttt{..\_window3}, \texttt{..\_window3\_vicreg}) --- the two SigReg variants from that
same checkpoint family are deliberately excluded here, since SigReg in this codebase
always regularizes $z_{\mathrm{agg}}$ (there is no token-granularity option for it the
way there is for VICReg), so evaluating it on the pre-aggregation tensor wouldn't be
testing what its own training objective actually targeted --- VICReg's
\texttt{vicreg\_granularity=token} setting, by contrast, was applied to exactly this
tensor, making it the directly comparable case.

$z$ was reduced to one vector per frame with a fixed, \emph{untrained} pooling instead of
the learned aggregation head: \texttt{mean} and \texttt{max} over the $P{=}196$ patch
dimension, and \texttt{concat} (flattening all patches into one $196{\times}8{=}1568$-dim
vector per frame --- the untrained counterpart of what the AggregationHead's MLP does
with a learned first layer). Computed fully in memory (\texttt{model.encoder(x)} once per
batch, all three poolings derived from that same forward pass); no intermediate feature
file written to disk. One methodological difference from the window/VICReg ablation
worth flagging directly: that experiment read each checkpoint at its own \emph{best}
epoch (16, 18, 43, 49); this one reads all four uniformly at \texttt{epoch49.ckpt}, so
the \texttt{window0\_2}/\texttt{window0\_vicreg\_3} columns here are not from the same
training step as their counterparts in that experiment (\texttt{window3}/\texttt{window3\_vicreg}
happen to coincide, since epoch 49 was already their best or near-best epoch there).
Scored with the same full metric suite from Section~\ref{sec:methodology} (Main and
Additional).

\paragraph{Inference}

\textbf{This directly answers the open question the window/VICReg ablation left
hanging, and the answer is: no, the encoder's own raw output was not collapsed ---
the aggregation head was the problem.} Under \texttt{mean} pooling,
\texttt{effective\_rank\_fraction} is 0.75--0.80 for all four checkpoints, uncolored
--- close to both DINOv2 baselines (0.73--0.81), nowhere near the 0.0155--0.0185
catastrophic collapse the exact same four checkpoints showed when read through
$z_{\mathrm{agg}}$ in the window/VICReg ablation. The raw per-patch encoder output was
carrying reasonable rank the whole time; the trained AggregationHead --- under-regularized
because VICReg's \texttt{token} granularity never actually reached it --- is what
collapsed it down to near-zero. This reframes that experiment's finding: it was never
really "this architecture collapses," it was "this specific aggregation step collapses
what it's given."

\textbf{Collapse status still depends heavily on which fixed pooling is used, and the
three poolings disagree with each other almost as much as trained vs.\ untrained
aggregation did.} \texttt{max} pooling makes \texttt{effective\_rank\_fraction} green for
all four checkpoints (0.86--0.96, clearly beating both baselines) --- squeezing to a
per-dimension maximum apparently spreads the 8 channel dimensions out more than either
mean or the trained aggregation head. \texttt{concat} pooling, which discards no
information at all (flattening all 196 patches to a 1568-dim vector), is red for all
four (0.36--0.45) --- worse than \texttt{mean}, despite retaining strictly more raw
information; effective rank is computed relative to the pooled dimensionality itself
($d{=}1568$ here vs.\ $d{=}8$ for mean/max), so a 1568-dim space needs proportionally
far more genuinely independent directions to score the same fraction. Collapse status
here is as much a property of the pooling choice as of the checkpoint.

\textbf{Every pooling reproduces the straightness-vs-temporal-order split seen
throughout this log, but \texttt{concat} is the only one where
\texttt{distance\_to\_goal\_spearman} turns green.} \texttt{trajectory\_straightness},
\texttt{mean\_velocity\_cosine}, and \texttt{direction\_reversal\_fraction} are green for
essentially every checkpoint under \texttt{mean} and \texttt{concat} (and green on
\texttt{direction\_reversal\_fraction} even under \texttt{max}, where
\texttt{trajectory\_straightness} itself is mixed-to-red), while
\texttt{time\_progress\_spearman} and \texttt{chirality.mean\_accuracy} are red
everywhere, all three poolings, all four checkpoints, no exceptions. But
\texttt{distance\_to\_goal\_spearman} breaks that pattern specifically under
\texttt{concat}: red under \texttt{mean} (0.44--0.50) and \texttt{max} (0.44--0.48), it
flips to green under \texttt{concat} (0.62--0.65, beating both baselines) for all four
checkpoints. Keeping every patch's own 8-dim projection, rather than compressing to one
vector via mean or max, is specifically what recovers the goal-distance signal here ---
the one metric in this whole log most directly tied to what a distance-minimizing
planner would actually use.

\textbf{VICReg's own contribution (\texttt{window0\_vicreg\_3} vs.\ \texttt{window0\_2},
\texttt{window3\_vicreg} vs.\ \texttt{window3}) is barely visible anywhere in this
table, evaluated on exactly the tensor \texttt{vicreg\_granularity=token} was supposed to
regularize.} Every metric pair sits within a few points of its non-VICReg counterpart at
the same window size and pooling --- no consistent, checkpoint-independent direction
either helping or hurting. This is a second, independent confirmation of the window/VICReg
ablation's core diagnosis: VICReg at token granularity was not doing meaningful work,
not just on $z_{\mathrm{agg}}$ (where it visibly failed to prevent collapse) but even on
the raw per-patch tensor it was actually applied to.
